Drip vs Soaker Hose — Recommendation

For your setup, the answer is use both. The C18Q's 18 output points let you mix individual drippers for large plants with seep hose runs for continuous rows. Here's why each method suits different parts of your bed:

Drippers — For Individual Large Plants

  • Delivers water directly to root zone — no foliar wetting
  • Prevents blight and fungal disease (critical for tomatoes in PNW's damp climate)
  • Precise control per plant — great for heavy feeders
  • Each dripper outputs ~1 liter/hour
Use for: Tomatoes (5), Hot Peppers (2), Basil (1)

Seep Hose — For Continuous Rows

  • Emits water along its entire length — even coverage
  • Perfect for densely planted rows (carrots, onions, lettuce)
  • Less installation work than individual drippers
  • Connects via dripper fitting (counts as 1 output per run)
Use for: Carrot/onion/herb rows, Cucumber runs, Pole bean trellis
Why not flat hose? The Irrigatia flat hose (IRR-SOAK, 25m) is designed for single-run coverage of long beds. It would work, but seep hose gives you more control over which zones get water — important since your bed has mixed crops with different needs. With seep hose you can route each run precisely where the plants are, skipping rhubarb and flower areas that don't need irrigation.

Installation Steps

Stage 1 — Set Up Barrel & Controller

  1. Place rain barrel at the east end of Plot A (rows 19–20 area), at least 30cm higher than the bed surface. South-facing side preferred for controller mounting.
  2. Mount the C18Q controller on the barrel or a nearby post/wall, south-facing for maximum solar exposure. Keep it above the barrel waterline.
  3. Drill the 5.5mm hole in the barrel lid. Thread the inlet tube through, attach the filter at the end. If using the water level sensor, make a second 10mm hole.
  4. Connect the water level sensor and filter using the cable ties provided. Middle sensor 2cm above filter, bottom sensor hanging below.
  5. Cut the outlet tube to reach from the pump port back to the bed. This becomes the start of your main feed tube.

Stage 2 — Run the Main Feed Tube

  1. From the controller's water outlet, run the main 3.5mm tube westward along the center of Plot A (approximately 2ft from the south edge — splitting the bed).
  2. Run the tube the full length of the bed, roughly 6m (~19.5ft) to the rhubarb end. The rhubarb doesn't need watering, so stop the tube around row 3.
  3. Install tee connectors where you need branches — roughly at rows 3, 5, 7, 9, 10, 12, 14, 16 along the main tube.
  4. At the western end of the main tube, insert a stopper.

Stage 3 — Install Tomato Drippers

  1. From tee connectors in the tomato zone (rows 9–18), run short branch tubes (20–40cm each) north to each tomato plant's root zone on the trellis side.
  2. Push a dripper into the end of each branch tube. Place the dripper at the base of each tomato plant, on the soil surface.
  3. Repeat for both hot peppers and the basil plant — 8 drippers total.
  4. Use stakes to hold each dripper and tube in position.

Stage 4 — Install Seep Hose Runs

  1. Onion row (south side, rows 3–10): Connect a ~2.4m length of seep hose via the dripper connector fitting. Run it along the south edge of the bed, through the onion row. Stopper at the far end.
  2. Carrot/herb row (center, rows 3–10): Connect a ~2.4m seep hose run through the center of the bed, weaving between the carrot and herb plantings.
  3. Pole bean trellis (north side, rows 2–8): Connect a ~2.1m seep hose run along the trellis side, watering the pole bean roots (tomatoes start at row 9).
  4. Cucumber south run (rows 11–19): Connect a ~2.7m seep hose run along the south edge, watering the cucumber row.
  5. South-center run (rows 11–19): Connect a ~2.7m seep hose run through the south-center, watering cucumbers, peppers, and basil.
  6. Total seep hose needed: ~12.3m. One 12m IRR-SH12 kit gets you very close — you may need a few extra inches or can slightly shorten the runs.

Stage 5 — Stoppers & Anti-Siphon

  1. Ensure every tube end has either a dripper, seep hose connection, or stopper. The system won't pressurize properly without this.
  2. Install the anti-siphon device on the outlet tube between the pump and the first dripper/tee — your bed is likely lower than the barrel, so this prevents back-draining when the pump stops.
  3. Push stoppers into the 5 unused output slots on the tube.

Stage 6 — Initialize & Test

  1. Follow Irrigatia's battery discharge/initialization process (press button to set 1d, wait ~2.5 hours).
  2. Once initialized, set to level 6 as a starting point. The pump will run every 3 hours during daylight.
  3. Check every dripper and seep hose run is flowing. Remove and re-insert any clogged drippers.
  4. After 2–3 days, let the air purge from the seep hose — it takes a few watering cycles to fully saturate.
  5. After a few days, adjust the level up (more water) or down based on soil moisture. Tomatoes and cucumbers are heavy drinkers in summer — you may end up at level 7–8 by July.

Recommended Settings for Issaquah, WA

The C18Q is weather-responsive — more sun means more water, which is perfect for your PNW climate where hot dry summers coincide with peak plant demand.

PeriodSet PointWhy
May (transplant)5–6Plants establishing, moderate water needs
June6–7Growth phase, longer days, increasing heat
July–August7–8Peak heat and fruiting — tomatoes and cukes drink heavily
September5–6Cooling down, shorter days, less demand
Internal switches: Leave all defaults except — if your barrel ever runs dry between refills, consider switching the beeper ON (switch 5) so the red LED alerts you. The water level sensor (switch 6, ON by default) will give you low-water warnings on the hand control module.
